Welcome to Iriji

The first yam of the season, shared with the whole community

Iriji is the Igbo New Yam Festival. It marks the end of the farming season and the moment when the first yams of the harvest may be eaten. Yam is the king of crops in Igbo land, so the day begins with thanksgiving to Chukwu, prayers led by elders, and the breaking and sharing of yam.

In Manchester the festival carries the same meaning. Families travel from across Greater Manchester and the rest of the United Kingdom to give thanks, greet elders, wear traditional dress, and pass the customs of home to children who are growing up here.

Everyone is welcome. You do not need to be Igbo to join us, only curious and ready to celebrate.

Four Pillars of Iriji

Festival, Real Highlights

Welcome to a richer cultural festival experience. Whether you are joining for the sacred yam ceremony, rhythmic masquerade dance, authentic cuisine, or kindred fellowship—choose your path below:

Culture & Tradition

The blessing of the new yam, sacred prayers by elders, coral beads, and isiagu worn with ancestral pride.

Music & Dance

Ogene drummers, traditional dance troupes, live masquerade performance, and music until dusk.

Food & Cuisine

Roasted and pounded yam, ji akwukwo, ugba, authentic nkwobi, smoky jollof, and small chops from local food stalls.

More Than a Festival

Culture is kept by being practised

A festival is how a community rehearses itself. When elders bless the yam in Manchester, when a child hears Igbo spoken by hundreds of people at once, when a town union dances in matching wrapper, the culture is not being remembered. It is being lived.

Language and custom passed to children born in the United Kingdom.
Town unions, women's groups and youth groups brought together on one field.
Igbo heritage shared openly with Manchester and its many other communities.
